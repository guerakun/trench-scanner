What it is

Trench Scanner looks at brand-new memecoins and sorts out which ones have better evidence of being safe and well-built, and which ones show warning signs.

It pulls public data (prices, trading, holders, security checks), gathers it into an evidence file for each coin, and asks Jev, an AI decision model, to grade it. Clear rules then double-check Jev's call and enforce hard safety limits.

It does not tell you what to buy. A grade ranks the evidence, not the future price. An A-grade memecoin is still a lottery ticket: most memecoins go to zero, even clean ones. Never put in money you can't afford to lose.

Quick start

Pick a mode

The chips under the title, like Default scan or Early hunt. Not sure? Use Default.

Tap Scan

Wait about 20 seconds. The green bar shows progress.

Read the cards

Start with the grade and verdict, then the Thesis and What we found. Tap a coin's Copy CA to grab its address.

That's all you need to start. The rest of this page explains the details.

Open the scanner
Seeing "Nothing met the bar"? That's normal and it's the tool working. Strict mode only shows A and B grades, and most launches don't earn one. Try again in a few minutes or switch modes.

Grades and verdicts

Verdict: is it safe to trade?

VerdictMeaning
PASSNo serious security problems found.
CAUTIONSome warning signs, such as concentrated holders or partly unlocked liquidity. Nothing that directly stops you selling.
FAILA serious problem, like a honeypot (you can't sell), high sell tax, or the creator being able to mint or freeze tokens. FAIL coins are hidden from the results.
UNKNOWNNo security checker covers this coin's chain, or the check failed. Unknown is never treated as a pass.

Grade: how strong is the evidence overall?

GradeMeaning
AStrong evidence across security, holders and liquidity, plus real activity.
BMostly clean, with a minor gap or one moderate weakness.
CMixed evidence, or capped by a safety rule (see below).
DSeveral weaknesses, or a security FAIL.
FSerious red flags.

About 80% of the grade comes from risk evidence (security, holder spread, how easy it is to sell, and whether the data checks out) and 20% from activity. For coins under a day old, activity means story and volume; for older coins it means crowd trend and volume.

How Jev decides, and how close calls are settled

Jev gives a probability for each grade. If one grade clearly leads, that's the grade. If the top two are within 10 points (say A 35% and B 35%), the scanner picks whichever one is closer to what the hard evidence says, and if that's still even, it takes the lower grade. If Jev is unsure overall, the scanner takes the more cautious answer. The card always tells you when this happened.

Safety caps: why a coin shows "Capped at C"

Some problems hold a coin at C or lower, whatever Jev says:

  • The security verdict is UNKNOWN.
  • The coin is under a day old and its liquidity is thin or not locked.
  • Another coin with the same ticker has deeper liquidity (copycats lose the name).
  • The creator has launched 5 or more other coins (a serial deployer).
  • The pump.fun bonding curve couldn't be verified on-chain.
  • Only a partial security report was available.
  • 10% or more of the supply sits in a lock vault, such as tokens the creator locked for themselves.
  • One wallet holds 10% or more of the supply, unlocked. It could sell at any time.

A security FAIL caps the grade at D, and hard failures like honeypots always force FAIL.

Scan modes

Each mode looks for a different kind of coin and remembers its own filter settings.

ModeWhat it findsGood for

Early huntUnder 24 hours old, up to $100K market capVery new, very small coins (highest risk)
Low cap$5K–$150K market capSmall coins of any age up to a week
FreshLaunched within the last hourWatching launches as they happen
Recovery$300K–$1M market cap, up to 7 days, trending and high-volume poolsEstablished coins that have held up

To stay within data-provider limits, each scan uses 5 of the market-data feeds and rotates the rest, so consecutive scans cover more ground. The status line says which feeds ran.

Filters and strict mode

Filters (tap "Filters" on a phone) let you set market cap, liquidity, age and minimum trades. Changes apply right away. Reset mode restores the mode's defaults.

Strict mode is on by default and shows only A and B grades. Turn it off to see everything that passed security, including C and D coins and why they were capped.

Read it left to right: coins found, coins actually trading, coins matching your filters, coins that didn't FAIL security, and coins on screen after strict mode and search.

Using it on a phone or with an AI agent

On a phone or another browser

On a device that's already set up, tap Copy setup link and send the link to yourself. Open it once on the new device. It saves the connection and starts a scan. After that, the normal address works there too.

Without the setup, cards say "Rules-only": the scanner still works, just without Jev.

With an AI agent

An agent that can use a web browser can open the setup link, wait for the scan to finish, and read the results as structured data. The steps are in the project README. An agent without a browser can use Copy results as JSON pasted into its chat.

Share the setup link carefully. Anyone who uses it spends your Jev and X credits (limited to 30 requests a minute per person). A setup link can't replace a connection that's already saved, so a bad link can't take over your scanner.

FAQ

Is this financial advice?

No. The scanner ranks evidence and flags risks. It never says buy or sell, and a good grade doesn't mean the price will go up.

Why does it say "Nothing met the bar"?

Strict mode shows only A and B grades, and most new coins don't qualify. That's the point: it saves you from looking at weak launches. Try another mode, wait a few minutes, or switch strict mode off.

What does "Rules-only" mean on a card?

Jev wasn't consulted for that coin, and the card says why: the device isn't set up (see Phone and AI agents), the coin failed security, or it was outside the top coins sent to Jev. The grade then comes from the rules alone.

Why is a coin "Capped at C"?

A safety rule applied, such as an unknown security verdict, thin liquidity on a new coin, or a copycat ticker. The card lists the exact reason. See Safety caps.

What is a lock vault, and why does it cap the grade?

A lock vault holds tokens that can't be sold until a set date. Creators often lock their own share to show they won't sell right away. The tokens still belong to one party, and they can be sold once the lock ends, so the scanner counts them as concentrated supply. For Streamflow vaults on Solana, the card says who gets the tokens, when they unlock, and whether the lock can be canceled. For other lockers, and for older vaults the free data source no longer has records for, it says the terms are unknown.

Why do some price changes show "–"?

The coin is younger than that time window. A 40-minute-old coin has no real 6-hour or 24-hour change, so those are left blank instead of repeating the 1-hour number.

Why is the verdict UNKNOWN?

No free security checker covers that chain, or the checker had no data yet. Unknown is never counted as safe, and it caps the grade at C.

Why does a scan take about 20 seconds?

The scanner checks security for dozens of coins, reads blockchain data and asks Jev about each candidate, all while staying within free data-provider limits.

Is the thesis true?

Not necessarily. It's the project's own words (or an X post), picked because it best explains what the coin is about. Treat claims about partnerships, apps or rewards as unverified.

Where is my data stored?

Your stars, mutes, scan memory and outcome log live only in this browser. Clearing your browser data or using a private window starts fresh.
